# Comparative Study on the Development Paths of US-Soviet Cooperative Engagement Systems: A Counterfactual Analysis Centered on the Capability of Separating Detection from Firepower

## Project Overview

This repository contains the thesis titled "Comparative Study on the Development Paths of US-Soviet Cooperative Engagement Systems: A Counterfactual Deduction Centered on the 'A Shoots, B Guides' System." The thesis compares the divergent development paths of two coordinated engagement systems under identical technological conditions, and employs counterfactual deduction to reconstruct the missing half of the comparison that was interrupted by the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.

### Core Research Question

At the end of the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union faced the same set of technological conditions: phased array radars had matured, digital data links had become practical, and air-to-air and surface-to-air missiles began to be equipped with active radar homing seekers. Why did the two sides ultimately pursue such different paths?

### Research Methods

- **Historical Comparison**: Reconstruct chronologically the technological conditions already possessed by both sides around 1991, including commissioned equipment, projects under development, and publicly disclosed technology roadmaps.
- **Counterfactual Deduction**: Following the operational norms proposed by Lebow, the deduction is constrained by three conditions: technological inheritance chains, engineering cycles, and continuity of system design philosophy, projecting both sides' system configurations to 2026 under a counterfactual scenario where the Cold War continued.
- **Bilateral Verification**: Cross-check the deduction results against the actual degeneration process of the Russian system after the Soviet dissolution.

### Key Findings

1. The gap in system concepts between the two sides in 1991 was far smaller than commonly portrayed; the Soviet air defense system already possessed the architectural skeleton of separated detection and fire control.
2. The US advantage lay primarily in the engineering capability to fuse geographically dispersed radar data into a single fire-control-quality track picture.
3. System design logic is an independent variable in explaining the outcome of this technological competition, distinct from the absolute level of technological capability.
4. When both sides possess second-strike capability and accurately assess each other's operational disadvantages, the net effect of coordinated engagement capability maturing synchronously between two superpowers may be to inhibit rather than encourage first-strike incentives.

**Keywords:** Cooperative Engagement Capability (CEC), Separation of Detection and Firepower, Counterfactual Reasoning, Offense-Defense Balance, US-Soviet Military Competition, Cold War Military History
